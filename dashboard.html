Field guide

Final Score Overall ranking from the scanner. This is the main field to trust first when comparing candidates.
Stock Price Current stock price. Use this to estimate how much capital you need to buy 100 shares.
Capital Required Estimated cash needed to buy 100 shares. This is usually the minimum capital needed for 1 covered call.
Premium Dollar premium per contract. Example: $250 means about $2.50 bid × 100 shares.
Weekly Income Estimate Estimated 1-week income from selling 1 contract. In this version it matches the premium.
Return on Capital Premium divided by capital required. Useful for comparing efficient income opportunities.
Delta Approximate pressure of expiring in the money. Lower usually means more cushion. Typical range for this strategy: 0.10–0.30.
Assignment Proxy Practical assignment-risk proxy using delta. Higher usually means greater chance of assignment.
OTM % Out-of-the-money percentage: how far the strike is above the stock price. Typical useful range: 3%–8%.
Annual Yield Premium normalized to a yearly rate. Comparison metric only, not a promise of yearly return.
Volume Number of contracts traded today for that option. Higher is usually better for fills.
Open Interest Number of existing open contracts. Higher usually means more liquid markets.
IV Percentile (Scan) Relative implied-volatility rank within this report. Higher often means richer premium versus other candidates today.
Current IV Current implied volatility estimate for that option. Higher IV often means richer premium.
Spread % Bid/ask spread relative to midpoint. Lower is better because it usually means easier fills.
Earnings Risk YES means the stock has earnings on or before expiration, which can heavily affect price and premium.
Expiration / Strike / Bid / Ask Supporting trade details for the specific call the scanner selected.
